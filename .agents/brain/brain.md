- Current dispatcher interfaces: `qpl.pricing.price` and `qpl.pricing.greeks`; both bind keyword arguments through a per-method `MethodSpec` and resolve the engine through the registry, rather than an `isinstance` ladder (ADR-0005). (source: src/qpl/pricing.py)
- Current registry exports: `qpl.engines.registry` exports `MethodSpec`, `EngineKey`, `register`, `resolve_price`, `resolve_greeks`, `method_spec`, `known_methods`. (source: src/qpl/engines/registry.py)
- Current instrument exports: `qpl.instruments` exports `VanillaOption`, `EuropeanOption`, `AmericanOption`, `DigitalOption`, `call_payoff`, `put_payoff`, `digital_payoff`. Exercise style is an instrument property: `EuropeanOption` and `AmericanOption` are sibling subclasses of the validation-only base `VanillaOption`, deliberately *not* parent and child, because registry lookup walks the MRO and a subclass would silently resolve to the European engines. Slice 6 adds `DigitalOption(kind, strike, expiry, cash=1.0)` -- a cash-or-nothing digital, **outside** that hierarchy entirely for the same MRO reason, sharing the kind/strike/expiry rules through the module-level `_validated_kind` helper rather than through inheritance. `cash` must be finite and `> 0`. `digital_payoff(S, K, cash, kind)` uses **strict** inequalities on both sides, so a point exactly on the strike pays nothing either way; that matches QuantLib's `CashOrNothingPayoff` and is what makes the oracle a comparison of the same contract. Slice 8 adds `AsianOption(kind, strike, expiry, fixing_times=(...), averaging='arithmetic'|'geometric')` plus `uniform_fixing_times(expiry, n_fixings)`, `arithmetic_average`, `geometric_average` and `asian_payoff`. `AsianOption` is **outside** both hierarchies for a third reason again: its payoff reads the *path*, not the terminal spot, so no engine that reduces the model to the terminal distribution can price it even in principle. Validation: `fixing_times` non-empty, finite, strictly increasing, all in `(0, expiry]`, normalised to a tuple of floats (a **repeated** fixing is refused -- it is a real contract but it makes the fixing grid and the simulation grid two different objects); `averaging` lower-cased into {'arithmetic','geometric'}. `uniform_fixing_times` uses `linspace` rather than `(i+1)*T/n`, because the latter lands one ulp **above** `T` at `T = 90/365, n = 2560` and `AsianOption` then correctly refuses a fixing after expiry. `geometric_average` is `exp(mean(log S))`, not an n-th root of a product, which overflows at 52 fixings. (source: src/qpl/instruments/__init__.py; src/qpl/instruments/options.py; src/qpl/instruments/payoffs.py)

- Current example-level interfaces: `qpl.engines.mc.pricers.MCConfig`, `price_european`, `greeks_european`. Slice 7 adds two backward-compatible `MCConfig` fields: `variance_reduction: str | tuple[str, ...] = "none"` (over `{"none", "antithetic", "control_variate", "stratified"}`, plus a tuple combining a sampler with the control variate) and `n_strata: int = 64`. `"none"` takes the pre-existing code path and is **bit-for-bit** what it was. `qpl.engines.mc.variance_reduction` exports `normalise_variance_reduction`, `validate_sampler`, `simulate_terminal_sample`, `estimate_from_sample`, `price_with_variance_reduction`, `terminal_spots_from_normals`, `control_variate_coefficient`, `TerminalSample`, `VarianceReductionEstimate` and the name constants `NONE`, `ANTITHETIC`, `CONTROL_VARIATE`, `STRATIFIED`. The three estimators are the ones in Glasserman (2003) chapter 4 and each carries **its own** standard error: `ddof=1` over antithetic *pairs* (not over the `2m` dependent payoffs), `ddof=2` regression residuals for the control variate (the coefficient is fitted on the same sample, so the estimator is biased at `O(1/N)` -- measured, order 1.06, against an `O(N^-1/2)` stderr), and the strata-weighted `sqrt(sum_i s_i^2 / (K^2 m))`. `PriceResult.meta` gains `variance_reduction`, `n_normal_draws` (the cost axis every ratio is measured on), `n_estimator_units`, and per estimator `n_antithetic_pairs`, `n_strata`/`paths_per_stratum`, or `control_beta`/`control_correlation`/`control_mean`/`control_sample_mean`/`control_variance_factor_predicted`. Both MC price paths (vanilla and digital) route through one shared entry point; `greeks_european` is unchanged because every bumped revaluation already goes through `price_european` with the same seed, so common random numbers survive. Measured factors and the five contradicted expectations: `docs/notes/mc_variance_reduction.md`. (source: src/qpl/engines/mc/pricers.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/variance_reduction.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/digital.py; examples/mc_variance_reduction.py)
- Slice 8 adds the Asian engines. `qpl.engines.analytic.asian` exports `discrete_geometric_price`, `geometric_average_log_moments`, `expected_geometric_average`, `expected_arithmetic_average`, `arithmetic_average_moments`, `turnbull_wakeman_price`, `price_asian`, `greeks_asian`. The geometric average is **exactly lognormal**: `log G ~ N(m, v)` with `m = log S + (mu - sigma^2/2) tbar` and the collapsed covariance sum `v = (sigma^2/n^2) sum_i (2(n-i)+1) t_i`, so the price is Black (1976) in `F = E[G]` with total variance `v` and `T` enters **only** through the discount factor (an average that ends before settlement therefore needs no special case). `turnbull_wakeman_price` is an explicitly-labelled approximation and is **deliberately not registered**: `price_asian` raises `NotSupportedError` on an arithmetic Asian naming it and Monte Carlo, so the registry never returns an approximation as if it were exact. Greeks raise on both routes. `qpl.engines.mc.asian` exports `price_asian`, `greeks_asian`, `asian_terminal_sample`; it samples the path at the fixing times only, so with exact lognormal stepping there is **no time-discretisation bias** and the only error is statistical. `variance_reduction='control_variate'` uses the geometric-average discounted payoff with its exact closed form as the control (Kemna & Vorst 1990), falling back to the discounted last-fixing spot for a geometric Asian where the geometric control would BE the payoff; estimation routes through the Slice 7 layer, and `TerminalSample` gained `control_name: str = "discounted_terminal_spot"` so a reported correlation says what it is a correlation with. `'stratified'` raises `NotSupportedError` naming Brownian-bridge stratification (stated in terms of the fixing schedule, because Slice 7's `n_steps > 1` rule would let a one-fixing Asian through), and `cfg.n_steps != 1` raises `InvalidInputError` rather than being silently ignored. `simulate_gbm_exact` gained an optional `times=` grid (exactly one of `(t, n_steps)` or `times`) with `gbm_paths_from_normals` as the shared kernel; the uniform branch is untouched and **bit-for-bit** what it was. Measured control-variate factors 1433 (10 fixings) and 1196 (52) against `1/(1-rho^2)` predictions of 1277 and 1262 at `rho = 0.9996`. Derivation, tables and the five contradicted expectations: `docs/notes/asian_options_control_variate.md`. - `PDEConfig` gained two fields in Slice 4, both defaulting to the previous behaviour. `time_stepping: Literal["theta", "rannacher"] = "theta"`: `"rannacher"` replaces the first **two** nominal steps by **four fully implicit steps of `dt/2`** (the module constant is `RANNACHER_STARTUP_STEPS = 4`) and then continues with `cfg.theta`; it requires `n_t >= 2`, takes `n_t + 2` steps, and covers exactly `n_t * dt` because halving is exact in binary floating point. `greeks_method: Literal["grid", "bump"] = "grid"`: `"grid"` reads delta and gamma from second-order central stencils on the finished grid, theta from the PDE identity, and vega/rho by bump-and-revalue on the same grid (`GRID_VEGA_BUMP = 1e-2`, `GRID_RHO_BUMP = 1e-4`), raising `InvalidInputError` at `T = 0` or `sigma = 0`; `"bump"` is the pre-Slice-4 three-solve spot bump (`BUMP_SPOT_FRACTION = 1e-2`) that still returns NaN for vega, theta and rho, kept as a named alternative and measured to stall at order 0.418 against the grid path's 2.001. `PriceResult.meta` adds `time_stepping`, `implicit_startup_steps` and `n_steps_taken`; `GreeksResult.meta` names the source of every Greek and carries `theta_backward_difference` as a first-order cross-check on the identity. Default `"none"` keeps vanilla and American output bit-for-bit. - Current American PDE exports: `qpl.engines.pde.american` exports `PSORConfig`, `price_american`, `greeks_american`, and documents `PSOR_OMEGA_DEFAULT`. Registered for `(AmericanOption, BlackScholesModel, "pde")` for both price and Greeks, so `method="pde"` is the second engine that can price early exercise. Each time step is the linear complementarity problem `A x - b >= 0, x - g >= 0, (x - g)(A x - b) = 0` with `A = I - theta dt L_h`, solved by **red-black projected SOR** -- even indices then odd, two vectorised numpy expressions per sweep, exactly a Gauss-Seidel sweep in a permuted order on a tridiagonal matrix. `PSORConfig(omega=1.2, tol=1e-8, max_iter=10_000, on_max_iter="raise"|"flag")`: `omega=1.2` is measured (the flattest sweep-count column over `n_s = n_t` from 100 to 1600, where the per-grid optimum drifts 1.00 -> 1.30), `tol` is **absolute** on the largest single-component change in a sweep, and an exhausted cap raises naming the step or is recorded in `meta["psor_unconverged_steps"]` -- never silently returned as converged. `PriceResult.meta` adds `psor_omega`, `psor_tol`, `psor_max_iter`, `psor_iterations` (read-only array), `psor_iterations_total`/`_mean`/`_max`, `psor_converged`, `psor_unconverged_steps`, `lcp_max_complementarity`, `lcp_min_constraint_slack`, `lcp_min_operator_residual`, `exercise_boundary`, `exercise_boundary_times` (reported explicitly because Rannacher makes the tau grid non-uniform), `early_exercise_node_count` and `exercise_time_level_count`. 4) Key invariants and assumptions
- `VanillaOption` (and so `EuropeanOption` and `AmericanOption`) requires kind in {"call","put"}, strike > 0, expiry >= 0; enforced at init. (source: src/qpl/instruments/options.py)
- `Market` requires spot > 0; enforced at init. (source: src/qpl/market/market.py)
- `BlackScholesModel` requires sigma >= 0; enforced at init. (source: src/qpl/models/black_scholes.py)
- Flat curves require non-negative rate/yield unless `allow_negative=True`. (source: src/qpl/market/curves.py)
- Dispatcher supports `EuropeanOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for methods {analytic, mc, pde, tree}, `AmericanOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for `tree` and `pde` (Slice 5), `DigitalOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for all four methods (Slice 6, with `method="mc"` pricing but refusing Greeks), and `AsianOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for `analytic` (geometric only) and `mc` (both averagings), each refusing Greeks with a message (Slice 8); otherwise `NotSupportedError`. Keyword validation runs before engine lookup, so a bad `cfg` yields `InvalidInputError`, not `NotSupportedError`. (source: src/qpl/pricing.py; src/qpl/engines/registry.py; tests/test_engine_registry.py)
- MC config requires n_paths >= 2 and n_steps >= 1; stderr uses ddof=1; results deterministic for a fixed seed. Slice 7 adds, checked at engine entry before any sampling (so a bad setting is rejected even at `T = 0`): `variance_reduction` must name known estimators without repeats, `"none"` cannot be combined with a real method, and `antithetic` + `stratified` is refused with `InvalidInputError` because reflecting a stratified draw lands it in the mirror stratum (symmetric stratification is a third sampler, not a combination). `antithetic` requires an even `n_paths`; `stratified` requires `n_steps == 1` -- `NotSupportedError` otherwise, naming Brownian-bridge stratification as the construction that does multi-step and a later slice as its home -- plus `1 <= n_strata <= n_paths`, `n_paths % n_strata == 0` (proportional allocation) and at least two paths per stratum (the within-stratum `ddof=1` variance). Slice 8 adds, on the Asian engine only: `n_steps` must be `1` (the time grid is the fixing schedule) and `stratified` is refused outright. (source: src/qpl/engines/mc/pricers.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/variance_reduction.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/asian.py; tests/test_mc_pricing.py; tests/test_mc_variance_reduction.py; tests/test_asian_mc.py)
- PDE config requires n_s >= 3, n_t >= 1, theta in [0,1], `strike_alignment` in {"none","midpoint"}, `time_stepping` in {"theta","rannacher"} with `n_t >= 2` for the latter, `greeks_method` in {"grid","bump"}, and `payoff_projection` in {"none","cell_average"}; deterministic for fixed inputs. The American PDE engine additionally requires `0 < psor.omega < 2` (Kahan's necessary condition for SOR, with the same message `sor_solve` uses), `psor.tol > 0`, `psor.max_iter >= 1` and `psor.on_max_iter` in {"raise","flag"}; those four are checked only on the American path, so a bad `psor` on a European call is ignored rather than rejected. PDE grid Greeks additionally require `T > 0` and `sigma > 0` (gamma is a point mass in both limits), matching the analytic engine; `price_european` still returns the correct price there. (source: src/qpl/engines/pde/pricers.py; tests/test_pde_pricing.py; tests/test_pde_greeks.py)
- Tree config requires n_steps >= 1 for prices and >= 2 for Greeks (gamma and theta read step-2 nodes), and scheme in {"crr", "leisen-reimer"}; `scheme="leisen-reimer"` additionally requires an odd n_steps, checked after the n_steps bound so `n_steps=0` still reports the size. The same applies to the American tree engine, whose Greeks use the same estimators (`lattice_delta_gamma_theta`). The CRR lattice raises `InvalidInputError` when no-arbitrage fails; the Leisen-Reimer lattice cannot violate it (`p' > p` follows from `d1 > d2`, and that is exactly `d < growth < u`). `lattice_delta_gamma_theta` takes an optional `spot`: pass it on a lattice that is not spot-centred, where theta needs an off-centre correction, and omit it on CRR, where the correction would only add round-off. Deterministic for fixed inputs. (source: src/qpl/engines/tree/pricers.py; src/qpl/engines/tree/lattice.py; tests/test_tree_pricing.py; tests/test_tree_leisen_reimer.py)
- At T=0 or sigma=0, European pricing returns intrinsic or discounted-forward intrinsic (analytic/MC/PDE/tree). Both American engines return intrinsic at T=0, and at sigma=0 the supremum of the discounted intrinsic value over the deterministic forward path, which equals `max(intrinsic now, discounted forward intrinsic)` whenever `r >= q` but **not** in general when `q > r` (derived and pinned: `qpl.engines.tree.american._deterministic_exercise_times`). The PDE engine imports that function rather than restating it, because it is a statement about the model and not about a discretisation; the tests therefore re-derive the value by a brute-force scan over exercise dates instead of comparing the two engines. (source: src/qpl/models/black_scholes.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/pricers.py; src/qpl/engines/pde/pricers.py; tests/test_pricing_analytic.py; tests/test_mc_pricing.py)
- Units: T in years; r and q are continuously compounded. (source: src/qpl/models/black_scholes.py; src/qpl/market/curves.py)

- Slice 2 delivered (Phase 1, second half): American exercise as an instrument type (`AmericanOption`, sibling of `EuropeanOption` under `VanillaOption`), American pricing and Greeks on the CRR lattice with the early-exercise boundary, measured order-1 convergence with the odd/even bracketing confirmed, and four findings that contradicted the slice's written expectations (the Longstaff-Schwartz Table 1 value is a 50-exercise-date Bermudan, the sigma=0 put formula needs r >= q, the extracted boundary is monotone only within a node-grid parity, and Richardson extrapolation does not restore order 2). Derivation: `docs/notes/american_exercise_on_trees.md`. (source: docs/CURRICULUM.md; .agents/brain/steering-brief.md)
- Slice 3 delivered (Phase 1, completing it): `TreeConfig(scheme="leisen-reimer")` for European and American vanillas and their Greeks, built from the Peizer-Pratt **method-2** inversion of the binomial tail at `d1` and `d2`. Measured European order 1.9840 at the money and 1.9709-1.9842 at the two off-money points where CRR's constant is erratic, with no oscillation and errors 507x to 5229x smaller than CRR's. Four findings that contradicted the slice's written expectations: the lattice Greeks are still order 1 (the estimators read levels 1 and 2, an `O(dt)` substitution no lattice fixes); the Greek *constants* improve for theta/vega/rho but not for delta/gamma, which land between CRR's two parities; the Leisen-Reimer vega error is flat at 3.06e-03 because `VEGA_BUMP`'s own `O(h**2)` bias is now the binding term; and the American order is 1.06, not 2, with Richardson still failing (1.34, residual 0.72). One real bug found: the shared theta estimator assumed `u d == 1` and does not converge on a Leisen-Reimer lattice without an off-centre correction. Derivation: `docs/notes/leisen_reimer.md`. (source: docs/CURRICULUM.md; .agents/brain/steering-brief.md)
- Slice 4 delivered (Phase 2, first item): `PDEConfig(time_stepping="rannacher")` and Greeks read off the grid (`greeks_method="grid"`), with delta/gamma/theta measured at order ~2 on an aligned grid and four contradicted expectations encoded -- the gamma pathology does *not* appear on the `n_s = n_t = n` refinement path (it needs `dt` large relative to `ds**2`, and on `n_s = 80 n_t` plain Crank-Nicolson fits a **negative** gamma order of -1.043 against Rannacher's 1.933); QuantLib's `FdBlackScholesVanillaEngine` does **not** damp by default and is 180x-1353x out on the same grids; theta from the last two time levels is first order, not second; and four half steps replace the first *two* nominal steps, not one. Also a `perf(pde)` commit moving the tridiagonal solve to `scipy.linalg.solve_banded` (7.4x-28.7x, worst price difference 7.2e-13). Derivation: `docs/notes/pde_greeks_and_rannacher.md`. (source: docs/CURRICULUM.md; .agents/brain/steering-brief.md)
- Slice 5 delivered (Phase 2, third item): American exercise by finite differences. `qpl.engines.pde.american` solves the per-step linear complementarity problem by red-black projected SOR and is registered for `(AmericanOption, BlackScholesModel, "pde")`; European output is bit-for-bit unchanged. Measured price order **1.8502** (residual 0.0265) against the lattice-bracketed limit, with the local orders already falling toward 1 as the free-boundary term takes over; the LCP is measured rather than assumed (slack exactly 0, complementarity at most 1.1e-07); three-engine agreement to 6.040e-04 at the ATM put. Five contradicted expectations: there is no single optimal `omega` (it is set by `dt / ds**2`, and is 1.00 at `n = 100`); sweep counts are flat on `n_s = n_t` and only grow when the stiffness ratio is isolated (exponent 0.787 on `n_s = 8 n_t`); the projection makes PSOR *faster*, not slower; the boundary has no parity structure to be monotone within, the Slice 2 caveat being a lattice artefact; and QuantLib's American FD engine is order 1.0328 against this one's 1.8502, with `dampingSteps` ruled out as the cause. Derivation: `docs/notes/pde_american_psor.md`. (source: docs/CURRICULUM.md; .agents/brain/steering-brief.md)
- Slice 6 delivered (Phase 2, fourth item): the cash-or-nothing digital as the discontinuous-payoff case. `DigitalOption` registered for all four methods; `PDEConfig(payoff_projection="cell_average")`; MC prices but refuses Greeks. Measured: a jump costs the finite-difference scheme a full order in the **price** (1.0012 unaligned, against 2 for a vanilla on the same grid), and four written expectations were contradicted -- Leisen-Reimer is order 2 on a digital rather than failing (and for an exact reason: the lattice price *is* the binomial tail its construction matches); midpoint alignment makes the cell-average projection a bit-for-bit no-op; alignment/projection and Rannacher are orthogonal and on a stressed grid both are required (plain CN diverges at delta order -1.00 and gamma -2.00); and the call+put identity breaks by 3.8% on an unaligned grid because a node sits exactly on the strike. Derivation: `docs/notes/digital_options_discontinuous_payoffs.md`. (source: docs/CURRICULUM.md; .agents/brain/steering-brief.md)
- Slice 7 delivered (Phase 3, the variance-reduction item): `MCConfig(variance_reduction=...)` with antithetic, a discounted-terminal-spot control variate and terminal stratification, each with its own standard error and each measured over 50 seeds at **equal normal draws**. Factors at K = 64 (predictions in brackets): ATM call 4.59 (4.01) / 7.58 (6.89) / 110.40, OTM call 3.04 (2.33) / 2.71 (2.30) / 42.41, ATM digital 9.32 (9.38) / 2.11 (2.45) / 101.26. Five contradicted expectations: antithetic is worth *most* on the digital (complementarity under reflection, not convexity); stratification gains `O(K)` and not `O(K**2)` on a vanilla (the unbounded tail stratum sets the floor, fitted 1.0157 and 1.0384); on a digital the gain is `K p(1-p)/(f(1-f))` and is **not monotone in K**; the combinations do not compose multiplicatively in either direction (92.35 against a product of 8.24 in one cell, and *worse* than one part alone in another); and stratification helps the CRN bump delta more than the price (variance factor 311 against 110) while adding the control variate to it makes delta worse. Coverage of the reported 95% interval 519/540 = 0.961. Derivation: `docs/notes/mc_variance_reduction.md`. (source: docs/CURRICULUM.md; .agents/brain/steering-brief.md)
- Slice 8 delivered (Phase 3, the Asian/control-variate item): `AsianOption` as the first **path-dependent** instrument, the discrete geometric average's exact closed form, and the Kemna-Vorst geometric control variate for the arithmetic average. Measured control factors 1433 (10 fixings) and 1196 (52) at `rho = 0.9996`, against 7.6 for the discounted terminal spot on a vanilla in Slice 7; discrete-to-continuous convergence measured at order **1** (1.0002 and 1.0096 in-repo, 1.0002 against QuantLib's continuous engine), derived beforehand from the `O(1/n)` corrections to `tbar` and `v`. Three published values reproduced (Clewlow-Strickland 5.3425606635 to 5.8e-11, Haug 4.6922 to 3.7e-05, Turnbull-Wakeman 19.5152 to 9.8e-06) and five contradicted expectations encoded -- the Haug value is a **put** on a 90/360 year fraction; the Turnbull-Wakeman point needs `q = r`; the estimator combinations **do** compose once the second factor is measured on the pair-averaged units (correcting the reading of Slice 7's finding); the control variate collapses to the plain estimator where nothing is in the money, and its confidence interval then fails to cover; and antithetic sampling is negligible next to the control here. Derivation: `docs/notes/asian_options_control_variate.md`. (source: docs/CURRICULUM.md; .agents/brain/steering-brief.md)
