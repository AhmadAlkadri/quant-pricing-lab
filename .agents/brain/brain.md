- Current instrument exports: `qpl.instruments` exports `VanillaOption`, `EuropeanOption`, `AmericanOption`, `DigitalOption`, `call_payoff`, `put_payoff`, `digital_payoff`. Exercise style is an instrument property: `EuropeanOption` and `AmericanOption` are sibling subclasses of the validation-only base `VanillaOption`, deliberately *not* parent and child, because registry lookup walks the MRO and a subclass would silently resolve to the European engines. Slice 6 adds `DigitalOption(kind, strike, expiry, cash=1.0)` -- a cash-or-nothing digital, **outside** that hierarchy entirely for the same MRO reason, sharing the kind/strike/expiry rules through the module-level `_validated_kind` helper rather than through inheritance. `cash` must be finite and `> 0`. `digital_payoff(S, K, cash, kind)` uses **strict** inequalities on both sides, so a point exactly on the strike pays nothing either way; that matches QuantLib's `CashOrNothingPayoff` and is what makes the oracle a comparison of the same contract. Slice 8 adds `AsianOption(kind, strike, expiry, fixing_times=(...), averaging='arithmetic'|'geometric')` plus `uniform_fixing_times(expiry, n_fixings)`, `arithmetic_average`, `geometric_average` and `asian_payoff`. `AsianOption` is **outside** both hierarchies for a third reason again: its payoff reads the *path*, not the terminal spot, so no engine that reduces the model to the terminal distribution can price it even in principle. Validation: `fixing_times` non-empty, finite, strictly increasing, all in `(0, expiry]`, normalised to a tuple of floats (a **repeated** fixing is refused -- it is a real contract but it makes the fixing grid and the simulation grid two different objects); `averaging` lower-cased into {'arithmetic','geometric'}. `uniform_fixing_times` uses `linspace` rather than `(i+1)*T/n`, because the latter lands one ulp **above** `T` at `T = 90/365, n = 2560` and `AsianOption` then correctly refuses a fixing after expiry. `geometric_average` is `exp(mean(log S))`, not an n-th root of a product, which overflows at 52 fixings. Slice 12 adds `BarrierOption(kind, strike, expiry, barrier, barrier_type='down-and-out'|'down-and-in'|'up-and-out'|'up-and-in', rebate=0.0, monitoring='continuous'|tuple)` plus `BARRIER_TYPES` and `uniform_monitoring_times`, **outside** every hierarchy for a fourth reason: the payoff reads the terminal spot *and* whether the running extremum ever reached a level, so it is path-dependent and discontinuous at once. It is the first instrument whose contract carries a **monitoring convention**, and that is deliberate: continuous and discrete monitoring are different contracts whose prices differ by `O(1/sqrt(m))`, so putting the convention on the instrument is what lets `method='analytic'` refuse a discrete schedule by inspecting the contract instead of quietly answering a different question. `barrier > 0` is validated at construction; its position relative to the **spot** is not, because an instrument here never reads a market -- `option.is_touched(spot)` resolves it at pricing time with *weak* inequalities, and a touched spot settles the contract at inception (knock-out -> rebate undiscounted, knock-in -> vanilla) identically in all three engines. The schedule rules are `AsianOption`'s, now in a shared `_validated_schedule` helper (same rules, same messages, one copy). - Slice 8 adds the Asian engines. `qpl.engines.analytic.asian` exports `discrete_geometric_price`, `geometric_average_log_moments`, `expected_geometric_average`, `expected_arithmetic_average`, `arithmetic_average_moments`, `turnbull_wakeman_price`, `price_asian`, `greeks_asian`. The geometric average is **exactly lognormal**: `log G ~ N(m, v)` with `m = log S + (mu - sigma^2/2) tbar` and the collapsed covariance sum `v = (sigma^2/n^2) sum_i (2(n-i)+1) t_i`, so the price is Black (1976) in `F = E[G]` with total variance `v` and `T` enters **only** through the discount factor (an average that ends before settlement therefore needs no special case). `turnbull_wakeman_price` is an explicitly-labelled approximation and is **deliberately not registered**: `price_asian` raises `NotSupportedError` on an arithmetic Asian naming it and Monte Carlo, so the registry never returns an approximation as if it were exact. Greeks raise on both routes. `qpl.engines.mc.asian` exports `price_asian`, `greeks_asian`, `asian_terminal_sample`; it samples the path at the fixing times only, so with exact lognormal stepping there is **no time-discretisation bias** and the only error is statistical. `variance_reduction='control_variate'` uses the geometric-average discounted payoff with its exact closed form as the control (Kemna & Vorst 1990), falling back to the discounted last-fixing spot for a geometric Asian where the geometric control would BE the payoff; estimation routes through the Slice 7 layer, and `TerminalSample` gained `control_name: str = "discounted_terminal_spot"` so a reported correlation says what it is a correlation with. `'stratified'` raises `NotSupportedError` naming Brownian-bridge stratification (stated in terms of the fixing schedule, because Slice 7's `n_steps > 1` rule would let a one-fixing Asian through), and `cfg.n_steps != 1` raises `InvalidInputError` rather than being silently ignored. `simulate_gbm_exact` gained an optional `times=` grid (exactly one of `(t, n_steps)` or `times`) with `gbm_paths_from_normals` as the shared kernel; the uniform branch is untouched and **bit-for-bit** what it was. - Slice 12 adds the **single barrier** and completes Phase 3. `qpl.engines.analytic.barrier` exports `barrier_price`, `barrier_blocks`, `BarrierBlocks`, `price_barrier`, `greeks_barrier`, `shifted_barrier`, `BGK_BETA` and `bgk_continuity_corrected_price`; the last is an explicitly-labelled approximation and is **deliberately not registered**, the same treatment `turnbull_wakeman_price` gets. The eight types are assembled from the six `A`-`F` blocks derived from the joint law of the terminal value and the running extremum (reflection principle plus Girsanov; Shreve II ch. 7), with Reiner & Rubinstein (1991) for the assembly and Merton (1973) for the down-and-out call. In-out parity is **exact** at zero rebate (1.42e-14 over twenty cells) and *false* with a rebate by exactly `E + F`, because the two rebate legs are paid on complementary events at different times. `greeks_barrier` is central differences of the closed form (`GREEK_BUMPS`), because a hand-derived version would need its own convergence evidence before beating a difference of a formula already checked to 1e-13 against QuantLib. `qpl.engines.mc.barrier` exports `price_barrier`, `barrier_terminal_sample`, `bridge_survival`, `normalise_barrier_correction` and `BARRIER_CORRECTIONS`; `MCConfig` gains `barrier_correction: Literal['none','bgk','brownian_bridge'] = 'none'`, read by this engine alone, and `meta['estimates']` says which of the two contracts the returned number estimates -- `'discrete'` for `'none'`, `'continuous'` for the other two. `'brownian_bridge'` weights each path by `prod_i (1 - p_i)` with `p_i = exp(-2 log(S_i/H) log(S_{i+1}/H) / (sigma^2 dt_i))`, which is `E[payoff 1{no touch} | sampled points]` and therefore **unbiased for the continuous contract at any m** -- measured at `m = 1`. `qpl.engines.tree.barrier` exports `price_barrier`, `boyle_lau_steps`, `barrier_layer_index`; a knock-in is assembled from three roll-backs on the *same* lattice, so in-out parity holds on the discretisation (1e-13 at `n = 51`) rather than only in its limit. Registry keys: `(BarrierOption, BlackScholesModel, m)` for `m` in {analytic, mc, tree} -- price and Greeks for analytic, price plus a registered-and-raising `greeks` for the other two. Measured orders: monitoring bias **0.5045** (paired 0.4603 +- 0.0125), CRR sawtooth amplitude **0.4566**, Leisen-Reimer 0.4542, Boyle-Lau subsequence **1.1258** (and 0.4254 when its step counts are handed to Leisen-Reimer). Six contradicted expectations and the QuantLib lattice finding: `docs/notes/barrier_options_monitoring_bias.md`. (source: src/qpl/engines/analytic/barrier.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/barrier.py; src/qpl/engines/tree/barrier.py; src/qpl/cases/barrier_black_scholes.py; examples/barrier_option_monitoring_bias.py)
- Slice 13 adds the **barrier PDE** and completes Phase 2. `qpl.engines.pde.barrier` exports `price_barrier`, `greeks_barrier`, `solve_leg`, `BARRIER_LEGS`, `KNOCK_OUT`, `KNOCK_IN`, `VANILLA_LEG`, and is registered for `(BarrierOption, BlackScholesModel, "pde")` for **price and Greeks** -- the first barrier engine here with real Greeks (the lattice's and the simulation's are registered-and-raising for reasons about *their* discretisations that do not apply to a grid whose barrier is a boundary), and the first engine of any kind that prices **both** monitoring conventions. Continuous monitoring truncates the domain at the barrier (`[H, s_max]` down, `[0, H]` up), so the Dirichlet condition sits on `S = H` exactly with no alignment arithmetic; discrete monitoring keeps the full domain and observes the barrier at the monitoring dates, on a time grid built as the sorted union of the `n_t` uniform levels and the `m` levels `T - t_j` (merged at `1e-12` relative, the monitoring value winning), so a projection never has to be interpolated onto a step. The knock-in is priced by **its own boundary problem** -- terminal data the rebate, `BS(H, K, tau)` imposed at the barrier, `rebate e^{-r tau}` at the far end -- and in-out parity is then a statement about the discrete system: the two legs and a third whose data is their sum share one matrix, so their prices add to 1.1e-16 -- 1.1e-15 **relative**, at any `n`. The monitoring projection is the `L2` one, weighted by the cell fraction with the live average read off the pre-observation `V` by linear interpolation; sampling it at nodes instead costs a full order and biases low (0.9619 against 2.2844, errors -6.34e-01 -> -8.60e-02 against +3.64e-02 -> +3.26e-04), which is the Slice 6 digital pathology once per monitoring date and is the slice's one contradicted expectation. Measured: continuous order **2.0668** on a node against **0.7079** off it (`|err| x n` in `[85.2, 245.7]`, all signs positive, 182x-4021x the aligned errors -- Zvan, Vetzal & Forsyth (2000), JEDC 24, 1563-1590); sinh/uniform error ratios 18.0 / 13.0 / 15.4 / 14.5; discrete-to-continuous gap order **0.4431** in `1/m` against the BGK shift's own **0.4361** on the same ladder, ratio 1.0147 -> 0.9915; grid delta/gamma orders 1.9980 / 1.9558 with Slice 12's gamma **sign flip** reproduced. `qpl.cases.barrier_black_scholes` grows to 26 rows (`BARRIER_PDE_ORDER_CASES`, `BARRIER_DISCRETE_CROSS_ENGINE_CASES`, plus `BARRIER_PDE_N`, `BARRIER_PDE_GRID`, `BARRIER_PDE_CONCENTRATION`, `BARRIER_PDE_STRIKE_ALIGNMENT`, `BARRIER_PDE_TIME_STEPPING`, `BARRIER_PDE_TOLERANCE`, `BARRIER_PDE_DISCRETE_STDERR_MULTIPLE`) and the continuous cross-engine rows become four-engine. Oracle finding: QuantLib's `FdBlackScholesBarrierEngine` is **order one** on exactly the contracts whose terminal payoff jumps across the barrier and order two on the rest, and `dampingSteps` is ruled out. (source: src/qpl/engines/pde/barrier.py; src/qpl/cases/barrier_black_scholes.py; tests/test_pde_barrier.py; tests/oracle/test_barrier_pde_vs_quantlib.py; examples/barrier_pde_grid.py; docs/notes/pde_nonuniform_grids_and_barriers.md)

4) Key invariants and assumptions
- `VanillaOption` (and so `EuropeanOption` and `AmericanOption`) requires kind in {"call","put"}, strike > 0, expiry >= 0; enforced at init. (source: src/qpl/instruments/options.py)
- `Market` requires spot > 0; enforced at init. (source: src/qpl/market/market.py)
- `BlackScholesModel` requires sigma >= 0; enforced at init. (source: src/qpl/models/black_scholes.py)
- `HestonModel` requires `v0 >= 0`, `kappa > 0`, `theta > 0`, `xi > 0` and `-1 < rho < 1`, all finite; enforced at init. `xi = 0` is refused on the dataclass (a deterministic-variance Heston is Black-Scholes with a time-dependent volatility) but accepted on the free function `heston_characteristic_function`, which returns the limit -- that is how the Black-Scholes reduction is tested as an identity on the formula rather than on a price. (source: src/qpl/models/heston.py)
- Flat curves require non-negative rate/yield unless `allow_negative=True`. (source: src/qpl/market/curves.py)
- Dispatcher supports `EuropeanOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for methods {analytic, fourier, mc, pde, tree}, `AmericanOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for `tree`, `pde` (Slice 5) and `mc` (Slice 11, price only -- its Greeks callable is registered and always raises), `DigitalOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for all four methods (Slice 6, with `method="mc"` pricing but refusing Greeks), `AsianOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for `analytic` (geometric only) and `mc` (both averagings), each refusing Greeks with a message (Slice 8), and `BarrierOption` + `BlackScholesModel` + `Market` for `analytic` (continuous monitoring only, price and Greeks), `mc` (discrete monitoring only, price; Greeks registered and always raising), `tree` (continuous monitoring only, price; Greeks registered and always raising) (Slice 12) and `pde` (**both** monitoring conventions, price **and** Greeks) (Slice 13); and `EuropeanOption`/`DigitalOption` + `HestonModel` + `Market` for `fourier`, price and Greeks (Slice 15); Slice 16 adds `method="mc"` for `EuropeanOption`, `DigitalOption`, `AsianOption` and `BarrierOption` under `HestonModel` (price for all four, Greeks for the European only -- the other three are registered-and-raising), so the remaining three methods (analytic, pde, tree) are the ones still registered for `HestonModel` with callables that always raise, naming the transform route rather than reporting an unsupported combination; otherwise `NotSupportedError`. Keyword validation runs before engine lookup, so a bad `cfg` yields `InvalidInputError`, not `NotSupportedError`. (source: src/qpl/pricing.py; src/qpl/engines/registry.py; tests/test_engine_registry.py)
- MC config requires n_paths >= 2 and n_steps >= 1; stderr uses ddof=1; results deterministic for a fixed seed. Slice 7 adds, checked at engine entry before any sampling (so a bad setting is rejected even at `T = 0`): `variance_reduction` must name known estimators without repeats, `"none"` cannot be combined with a real method, and `antithetic` + `stratified` is refused with `InvalidInputError` because reflecting a stratified draw lands it in the mirror stratum (symmetric stratification is a third sampler, not a combination). `antithetic` requires an even `n_paths`; `stratified` requires `n_steps == 1` -- `NotSupportedError` otherwise, naming Brownian-bridge stratification as the construction that does multi-step and a later slice as its home -- plus `1 <= n_strata <= n_paths`, `n_paths % n_strata == 0` (proportional allocation) and at least two paths per stratum (the within-stratum `ddof=1` variance). Slice 8 adds, on the Asian engine only: `n_steps` must be `1` (the time grid is the fixing schedule) and `stratified` is refused outright. Slice 12 adds the same `n_steps == 1` rule on the barrier engine (the grid is the monitoring schedule), refuses `stratified` with a message that distinguishes the Brownian-bridge *sampler* from `barrier_correction='brownian_bridge'`, refuses continuous monitoring, refuses `barrier_correction='bgk'` on a non-uniform schedule (`beta sigma sqrt(dt)` names a single `dt`) and refuses a **knock-out** rebate under the bridge (the rebate is paid at the touch time and the bridge supplies the crossing probability, not its date; a knock-in rebate is paid at expiry and is supported). (source: src/qpl/engines/mc/pricers.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/variance_reduction.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/asian.py; tests/test_mc_pricing.py; tests/test_mc_variance_reduction.py; tests/test_asian_mc.py)
- PDE config requires n_s >= 3, n_t >= 1, theta in [0,1], `strike_alignment` in {"none","midpoint"}, `time_stepping` in {"theta","rannacher"} with `n_t >= 2` for the latter, `greeks_method` in {"grid","bump"}, `payoff_projection` in {"none","cell_average"}, `grid` in {"uniform","sinh"}, `concentration` finite and `> 0`, `grid_points` `None` or a non-empty tuple of finite positive levels, and `barrier_alignment` in {"node","none"}; deterministic for fixed inputs. The barrier PDE engine additionally refuses Greeks when the spot has already touched a continuously monitored barrier (the contract is settled at inception, so there is no grid to differentiate). The American PDE engine additionally requires `0 < psor.omega < 2` (Kahan's necessary condition for SOR, with the same message `sor_solve` uses), `psor.tol > 0`, `psor.max_iter >= 1` and `psor.on_max_iter` in {"raise","flag"}; those four are checked only on the American path, so a bad `psor` on a European call is ignored rather than rejected. PDE grid Greeks additionally require `T > 0` and `sigma > 0` (gamma is a point mass in both limits), matching the analytic engine; `price_european` still returns the correct price there. (source: src/qpl/engines/pde/pricers.py; tests/test_pde_pricing.py; tests/test_pde_greeks.py)
- Tree config requires n_steps >= 1 for prices and >= 2 for Greeks (gamma and theta read step-2 nodes), and scheme in {"crr", "leisen-reimer"}; `scheme="leisen-reimer"` additionally requires an odd n_steps, checked after the n_steps bound so `n_steps=0` still reports the size. The same applies to the American tree engine, whose Greeks use the same estimators (`lattice_delta_gamma_theta`). The CRR lattice raises `InvalidInputError` when no-arbitrage fails; the Leisen-Reimer lattice cannot violate it (`p' > p` follows from `d1 > d2`, and that is exactly `d < growth < u`). `lattice_delta_gamma_theta` takes an optional `spot`: pass it on a lattice that is not spot-centred, where theta needs an off-centre correction, and omit it on CRR, where the correction would only add round-off. Deterministic for fixed inputs. (source: src/qpl/engines/tree/pricers.py; src/qpl/engines/tree/lattice.py; tests/test_tree_pricing.py; tests/test_tree_leisen_reimer.py)
- At T=0 or sigma=0, European pricing returns intrinsic or discounted-forward intrinsic (analytic/MC/PDE/tree). Both American engines return intrinsic at T=0, and at sigma=0 the supremum of the discounted intrinsic value over the deterministic forward path, which equals `max(intrinsic now, discounted forward intrinsic)` whenever `r >= q` but **not** in general when `q > r` (derived and pinned: `qpl.engines.tree.american._deterministic_exercise_times`). The PDE engine imports that function rather than restating it, because it is a statement about the model and not about a discretisation; the tests therefore re-derive the value by a brute-force scan over exercise dates instead of comparing the two engines. (source: src/qpl/models/black_scholes.py; src/qpl/engines/mc/pricers.py; src/qpl/engines/pde/pricers.py; tests/test_pricing_analytic.py; tests/test_mc_pricing.py)
- `qpl.engines.mc.sde.simulate` requires `n_paths >= 1`, a finite `x0` at or above the model's `state_floor`, a strictly increasing `t_grid` starting at exactly `0.0`, exactly one of `seed`/`rng`/`normals` (a `normals` block of shape `(n_paths, len(t_grid) - 1)`), `scheme` in `SCHEMES` and `truncation` in `TRUNCATIONS`. `truncation='full'` needs a model with a `state_floor` and is refused for `scheme='exact'` rather than ignored, because a silent no-op there would read as if the exact sampler needed fixing. `scheme='milstein'` needs a diffusion derivative from one of three places or raises naming `db_dx`. Deterministic for a fixed seed, for every scheme including the CIR `ncx2` sampler. CIR parameters must all be strictly positive; the scheme is exact on **both** sides of the Feller condition, since `scipy.stats.ncx2` needs only `df > 0`. (source: src/qpl/engines/mc/sde.py; tests/test_mc_sde.py)
- Units: T in years; r and q are continuously compounded. (source: src/qpl/models/black_scholes.py; src/qpl/market/curves.py)

6) Configuration & defaults
- MC defaults: `MCConfig(n_paths=50_000, n_steps=1, seed=123, variance_reduction="none", n_strata=64, greeks_estimator="bump", heston_scheme="qe", heston_conditional=False)`. The two Slice 16 fields are read by the Heston engines alone; note that the default `n_steps=1` is **refused** there, because under Heston `n_steps` is the time discretisation and no scheme is exact in one step. `greeks_estimator` is read by the Greeks entry points only and defaults to the pre-Slice-10 behaviour. Both Slice 7 fields default to the pre-slice behaviour; `n_strata=64` is read only when `stratified` is selected and is not a tuned value (the gain on a vanilla grows like `K`, so there is no optimum, and on a digital it depends on where the jump falls between stratum boundaries). (source: src/qpl/engines/mc/pricers.py)
- PDE defaults: `PDEConfig(n_s=200, n_t=200, theta=0.5, s_max=None, s_max_multiplier=4.0, strike_alignment="none", time_stepping="theta", greeks_method="grid", payoff_projection="none", grid="uniform", concentration=0.05, grid_points=None, barrier_alignment="node", psor=PSORConfig())`. The four Slice 13 fields all reproduce the pre-slice behaviour: `grid="uniform"` is bit-for-bit the old mesh, and `concentration`/`grid_points`/`barrier_alignment` are read only when `grid="sinh"` or by the barrier engine. `concentration=0.05` is **not** a tuned optimum -- on the Slice 13 barrier point 0.02 is 1.2x better and on a plain vanilla every value is worse than uniform, with `PSORConfig(omega=1.2, tol=1e-8, max_iter=10_000, on_max_iter="raise")`. The `slow` marker is registered in `[tool.pytest.ini_options] markers` and applied by a **measured** rule: a test function is `slow` when its worst individual case (setup + call + teardown, read off `pytest --durations=0`) exceeds about 3 s; parametrised functions carry the mark as a whole, because a mark on one generated id does not survive a change to the parameter list.
